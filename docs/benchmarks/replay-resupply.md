## What happened, in integers

1. The attack contract sent 2,000 crvUSD straight to the cvcrvUSD vault's controller (a donation: no shares minted). Before the transaction the vault had 0 shares and 0 assets; the oracle read 1,000,000,000,000,000 (= 1e18 / DEAD_SHARES) and the pair's stored exchange rate was 1,000,000,000,000,000,000,000. The pair had been deployed 95 minutes earlier (block 22784988); the vault 40.1 days earlier (block 22499073), and it held no shares and no assets.
2. It minted 1 share wei for 2,000,000,000,000,000,001 crvUSD wei (Vault.vy:422 rounds the cost up), so the vault held totalSupply = 1, totalAssets = 2,002,000,000,000,000,000,001 wei.
3. It posted that 1 share wei as collateral and called `borrow(10,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000, 0, …)` (10,000,000 reUSD, all of the pair's 10,000,000 available).
4. Inside `borrow`, `BasicVaultOracle.getPrices` returned **2,000,000,000,000,000,000,001,998,001,998,001,998** (convertToAssets(1e18) = 1e18 × (totalAssets + 1) / (totalSupply + 1000), floored).
5. `_exchangeRate = 1e36 / price` = **0** (the transaction emitted `UpdateExchangeRate(0)`). The exact quotient is 0.4999999999999999999995004995.
6. The solvency check: borrower amount 10,000,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 × rate 0 × 1e5 / 1e18 / 1 = LTV **0** ≤ maxLTV 95,000 (95%): solvent. 10,000,000 reUSD were minted against 1 wei of cvcrvUSD.

With the exact rate the same check computes LTV = 499999999999.9999999995004995 in units of 1e-5: 5,263,158 times the 95% cap. The most the 1-wei position could owe at the cap is 1.9 reUSD (one share wei is worth 2.000000000000000000002 crvUSD).

## The decisive step on each ruler

Same integers, same formula (`ResupplyPairCore.sol:573` then `:282`), different arithmetic. *rate* is collateral wei per 1e18 reUSD wei; *LTV* is in units of 1e-5 (maxLTV 95,000); *max borrow* is the reUSD the 1-wei position may owe at maxLTV, rounded down (the protocol's favour); *digits* is −log10 of the relative error against the exact value.

Reading the table: the deployed rate has 0 correct digits (it is 0 where the truth is one half). `divUp` blocks the attack but is a one-mark ruler too: it reads one half as 1, doubling the LTV and halving what an honest borrower could take (0.95 reUSD instead of 1.9). Dividing by the price at the end needs no new library and gets the verdict and the LTV right to the integer. FP127 and FP256 carry the half and give the exact verdict and the exact cap.

Range: FP127 holds integers below 2^127 ≈ 1.7e38, so its port of this line takes the price as an exact integer only while totalAssets stays under 170311.325 crvUSD with one share outstanding (85 times the attack's); above that the port reverts (`_q` in the test) rather than floors. FP256 has no such wall.
