# FP256 in Yul

256-bit binary floating point for the EVM. Release four, live on Sepolia
since 2026-10-21 (Blockulator 2.1): 67 ops, every one correctly rounded or
exact, in four deployed objects of 24,512 (FP256), 22,266 (FP256X), 19,889
(FP256X2) and 6,844 (FP256X3) bytes from one source (release 2.2 adds a
fifth, FP256X4, for the error function family); addresses below and in
[`contracts/deployments/FP256.md`](../../deployments/FP256.md). Releases one
to three (release three: 28 ops, one object) stay deployed.

One `int256` per value, the bit pattern of a float:

| bits | field |
|---|---|
| 255 | sign |
| 254..236 | biased exponent E, 19 bits, bias 262,143; valid 1..524,287 |
| 235..0 | fraction F; the significand is 2^236 + F, 237 bits |

value = (−1)^s · (2^236 + F) · 2^(E − 262143 − 236). Zero is the all-zero
word and the only word with E = 0; any other word with E = 0 is not a
number and every op reverts `OutOfRange` on it. There is no NaN, no
infinity, no subnormal and no negative zero: every valid word is a finite
number with one representation, equality is bit equality, and positive
words order as unsigned integers. Range 2^−262142 to just under 2^262145,
about 10^±78,913; 237 significant bits, about 71 decimal digits, at every
magnitude.

This is the second ruler. [FP127](../fp127/README.md) is a fixed point:
38 digits at 1, 8 at 10^−30, and nothing outside 2^−128 to 2^127. FP256 keeps 71
digits wherever the value sits, at the price of an exponent to align on
every add and a renormalisation on every result. The
[edges](../../../docs/benchmarks/edges.md) are why it exists.

The split is by family, as
[#79](https://github.com/securedataresearch/blockulator/issues/79) asks:
FP256 is what a protocol calls at the token edge and in accrual, FP256X the
special functions, FP256X2 the rest of the exponential family on the same
kernels (cbrt is Newton from a seed in the bits), FP256X3 the algebraic
ops. FP256 has 64 bytes left and FP256X 2,310, so #79's 25 ops, 25,479
bytes in one object, are two more. Release 2.2 moves the error function
family to FP256X4: its node table and two-word second stage (about 13 KB
with the kernels they share) no longer fit beside Lambert W. `make
build-fp256` compiles all five, `make size-fp256` prints them, and
`predict-fp256x`, `-fp256x2`, `-fp256x3`, `-fp256x4`
with their `deploy-...-sepolia` and `verify-...-sepolia` mirror FP256's
targets.

## Rounding

**To nearest**, half away from zero on the magnitude, once per op, for every
op without a direction in its name. Every rounding site is `normround`: the
exact result is floored on a grid at least as fine as half an ULP, then the
first discarded bit is added. That keeps every op odd-symmetric
(`neg(add(a, b)) == add(neg a, neg b)`, bitwise) and needs no sticky bit.
Overflow (an exponent above 524,287 after rounding) reverts `Overflow`;
underflow rounds to zero with the sign dropped.

**Directed** (release four), for the token edge: a protocol converting a
value into token units must round toward itself. In the Balancer V1 replay
([`replay-balancer-v1.md`](../../../docs/benchmarks/replay-balancer-v1.md))
exact maths rounded to nearest still lost 7% per cycle. `Down` rounds toward
minus infinity and `Up` toward plus infinity, once, from the exact value:
`dirround` floors the magnitude and adds one ULP when any bit was dropped
(the product's low bits, the remainder `mulmod(ma, 2^255, mb)`) and the
direction points away from zero. A product or quotient below the format is
zero toward zero and the smallest number (2^−262142, with its sign) away
from it, so `mulUp` never rounds a positive amount to zero; `toUintUp` and
`toFixed18Up` charge one unit for any positive dust.

**Integral values** (#79): `round` takes halves away from zero, FP256's
tie rule (FP127's `round` takes them up); `frac` is x − floor(x) rounded
once, so a negative x within 2^−238 under an integer gives exactly 1.

## Correct rounding

Every op that is not exact by construction computes its fast value D (the
input of `normround`) as in release three, and a bound err on |D − exact|
in units of D's last bit. `rbad(D, err)` asks whether D ± err can round
differently from D: the bits under the 237-bit significand within err of
half an ULP. Only then a second stage recomputes the value in two words at
2^−510 (`(h, l)`, h a kernel word at 2^−254) and rounds that.

The second stage:

- `wexpm1`: e^r − 1 for r in [0, 1): z = r/2^24, e^z − 1 = z·T(z) by 19
  Horner terms (k = 20..12 in one word, k = 11..2 in two), then 24
  doublings E ← 2E + E² on the value scaled by 2^24, which keep its
  relative precision; within 2^−503. The doublings run in one loop without
  calls, each square with its cross term formed once (`wdbl`, bit for bit
  the same as `wmul`'s), so a call costs about 21k gas, not 27k.
- `wexp`: e^x = 2^k(1 + E), r = x − k ln 2 exact modulo 4 at 2^−510 with
  ln 2 in three words.
- `wlnw`: ln of a mantissa in [1, 2) by one Newton step from the kernel's
  value (within 2^−249): y0 − (e^y0 − m)/e^y0, the residual at 2^−510 and
  its quotient at 2^−502; within 2^−497.
- `wln1ps` and `em1w`: the series ln(1 + d)/d (⌈512/(−u − 1)⌉ terms for
  |d| < 2^(u+1): 128 at most, 64 below 2^−8, 14 at 2^−40) and (e^x − 1)/x
  in two words, relative, for the near-one bands.
- pow and compound: log2 x (or log2(1 + rate)) in two words, its exact
  three-word product with y's significand (or with n, any int256), split at
  2^−510, 2^f by `wexpm1`. ln, log2 and log10 hand the second stage the
  kernel's log2m they already have. powu, when its own bound misses, goes
  straight to pow's second stage (pow's looser bound would miss too);
  compound skips its fast value where its bound is sure to reach the
  rounding boundary (`compsure`: a per-second rate with |n log2(1 + rate)|
  from about 11,000 to 2^19).
- The error function: the same trapezoidal rule as the fast path (below)
  with 113 nodes in two words, truncation below 2^−502 (the theorem at
  N = 112; measured 2^−511, `bounds.py`): the node weights by two
  recurrences c_(k+1) = c_k ρ_k, ρ_(k+1) = ρ_k e^(−2h²), each denominator
  y² + t_k² normalised and inverted by `wrecip`, e^(−y²) by `wexpnsq`, the
  pole term 2/(1 + e^(2πy/h)) by `wexpr` (dropped from y = 17, where it is
  under 2^−500 of erfc); within about 2^−490 of erfc. erf below y = 2^−5 is
  tanh(πy/h) − e^(−y²) y U with tanh = (E − 1)/(E + 1) from the same E =
  e^(2πy/h) = 2^k(1 + F): E − 1 is F itself when k = 0, so it keeps its
  relative precision down to y = 2^−130 (2^−380 of erf at 2^−129, where
  `wexpm1`'s 2^−503 floor begins to show), and below that erf(y) lies between
  (2/√π)(y − y³/3) and (2/√π) y (the integrand e^(−t²) is between 1 − t² and
  1), within 2^−262 of (2/√π) y, which is the value.
- Lambert W: from FP127's seeds (their logarithms by a degree-7 fit, 2^−21),
  fourth-order steps at word precision on w + ln(w/x) = 0, the series
  reversion w(1 − u + uv/2 − uv²/2 + u²v/3) with u = f/(1 + w), v =
  u/(1 + w), the logarithm of the ratio of the significands by the log2
  kernel (one or two steps, three from Winitzki's seed below 2^64, of which
  the first runs on the fitted logarithm); the step that starts within
  2^−66 has a bound, 2·(1.01·du + 5) + 1 with du = 1 + 32·2^254/|h|, and
  `rbad`. Below p = 2^−21 the branch-point series W = −1 + Σ μ_k p^k to
  k = 11 (the next term under 2^−261) by Horner at 2^−254, within 2.2
  units, and `rbad(D, 4)`. On a miss one log-form Newton step at 2^−510, w − (w + ln(|w|/|x|))·w/(1 + w). W and x
  share a sign, so the form holds for every x ≠ 0, and the ratio's one
  wide ln keeps the correction's relative precision down to the smallest
  x.

#79's second stages:

- `exp2` and `exp10`: x or x log2 10 (three words) split at 2^−510 and 2^f
  by `wexpm1`, as pow's (`pow2w`).
- `log2`: pow's wide log2 (`wlog2`: the wide series near one, wln off it).
  `log10`: u log10 2 in three words plus ln(mantissa) log10 e (wln, then a
  wide product), or the wide ln series times log10 e near one.
- `sinh` and `cosh` from 1/2: e^|x| = 2^k (1 + E) by `wexp` and e^−|x| by
  `wrecip`, summed or subtracted in two words; below 1/2 `sinh` is x times
  sum x^2k/(2k + 1)! in two words. `tanh`: (1 − w)/(1 + w) with w =
  e^−2|x| by `wexp`, or the two series' quotient below 1/2. (The fast
  paths below 1/2 compose sinh and tanh from expm1's one-word machinery,
  and atanh below 2^−6 from log1p's; only the second stages keep a
  series of their own.)
- `atanh`: below 2^−4, x sum x^2k/(2k + 1) in two words; above, (uu1 −
  uu2) ln 2 plus wln of the exact mantissas of 1 + |x| and 1 − |x|, three
  words, halved in the one rounding.

Exact by construction: `cbrt` (the floor of the cube root of the
significand scaled to 714 to 716 bits, by Newton with precision doubling from a
seed in the bits and an exact three-word cube at the end), `gavg` and `hypot` (the floor of the square root of the
exact 474-bit product or sum of squares, floored only on a grid far under
the root's, so hypot's Pythagorean ties, which exist, round away from zero),
`lerp` (the exact a + t (b − a), below), `avg`, `dist`, `zeroFloorSub`,
`inv` (one rounding of an exact sum, difference or quotient), `min`, `max`,
`clamp`, `round`, `log2Up`, `gcd` (exact values), `pi` (a table of
correctly rounded constants) and `factorial` (exact while n!'s odd part fits
512 bits, so every exact tie; a 512-bit running product within 2^−500
above, checked against the exact factorial at every n of its domain).

`lerp` sums its three exact terms, a, t b and −t a, in a 1,024-bit window
under the largest. A term whose last bit falls under the window enters as
its floor; with one such term the floor of the sum is exact and lies at
least 544 bits under the sum (or the other two cancel exactly and the term
is the value); two such terms lie more than 546 bits under the third, which
fixes the result up to the sign of their sum. The window is four words of
memory just past the free memory pointer, which lerp reads and never moves
(memory-safe temporary memory), and the three terms, computed once, are
the fifteen words after it (sign, magnitude, last-bit exponent and top
position each). The error function family uses the same kind of memory:
the fast path's 59-word node table and four words of sum and y², the
second stage seventeen words.

pow, compound and powu compute exact ties exactly: when x^y has at most 238
significant bits (y = n/2^j with j ≤ 7 and n ≤ 150, the odd part of x a
perfect 2^j-th power: `powtie`; the odd part of 1 + rate for compound:
`onepexact`) the power is formed as an integer and rounded once. For the
other functions an exact tie cannot occur where a theorem says so: e^x, ln x
and W(x) are transcendental at every dyadic x other than 0 and 1
(Lindemann–Weierstrass). For erf, erfc and Φ none does; a tie would need a
dyadic value of exactly 238 bits.

`scripts/fp256/generators/bounds.py` recomputes each D as the Yul does (the
kernel254.py and erf256.py models) against 120-digit truth: no measured
error reaches 0.67 of its bound (the error function family's reach 0.22;
their bounds are static sums of positive terms' roundings). An input misrounds only if its exact value lies
within the last column's distance of a tie: for a value without structure,
a chance of about twice that distance per input, 2^−116 at worst (next to
Lambert W's branch point). None is known.

## Precision

`make test` runs every op over the committed vectors in
`test/fp256/vectors/` (generated by `scripts/fp256/oracle.py`: exact
rationals for the arithmetic, the conversions and the directed ops, exact
powers for the ties of pow, compound and powu, mpmath at 300 digits for the
rest) and writes `docs/fp256/precision.json`; `make precision-report-fp256`
renders [`docs/fp256/precision.md`](../../../docs/fp256/precision.md). Every
op is held to the word itself at every vector, ties included: pow(c, 3),
pow(c², 3/2), powu(−c, 3) and compound(c − 1, 3) for c = 2^79 + 1, whose
cube has exactly 238 bits, and 3^150.

`make sweep` ([`docs/benchmarks/sweep.md`](../../../docs/benchmarks/sweep.md))
runs 364,658 seeded random inputs through the ops, each compared with
the correctly rounded word: 0 misrounded (`factorial` at every n of its
domain). The first 4,000 inputs of each
correctly rounded op go again through a test-only copy of the library whose
rounding test always fails, so every result comes from the second stage: 0
misrounded there too.

Exact ties in truncated series need care: 1 + x + x²/2 is a tie whenever
x = a·2^−118 with a odd, so `exp` floors 1 + x + x²/2 exactly on the
2^−254 grid below 2^−118 (the cubic only breaks the tie). The series bands
of `ln`, `log1p` and `expm1` meet the same ties (x ± x²/2), and their
remainder x³/3 is at least 2^−236 ULP there, well inside what the second
stage resolves.

## What changed from FP127's conventions

`pow(0, y)` with y < 0 reverts `DivisionByZero` (FP127 returns 0). The
comparisons return plain integers, not numbers. #79's ops keep FP127's names
and signatures with float semantics: `round` takes halves away from zero
(FP127: up), `frac` can return 1 (above), `gcd` and `log2Up` return
numbers, `factorial` runs to 20,366 (FP127: 33), `pi(terms)` returns the
correctly rounded partial sum rather than computing it in the format, and
`lerp`, `avg`, `dist`, `hypot` and `gavg` round once from the exact value
instead of after each step. Everything else follows
FP127: the same three errors, the same generator, the same fuzzing of the
objects and the library for agreement across the full `int256` domain
(`test/fp256/Equivalence.t.sol`), and the directed ops carry the names
FP127's release five uses (mulDown, mulUp, divDown, divUp, toFixed18Down,
toFixed18Up).
